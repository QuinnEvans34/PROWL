## Why can a pancreatic tumor blend in with the surrounding tissue on a CT scan?

Case: q:dev2-01

**Expected meaning**

- Some pancreatic tumors resemble surrounding tissue in CT density or intensity, producing weak contrast that makes their boundaries difficult to distinguish.

**Limit:** General imaging explanation; do not diagnose a displayed lesion, claim every tumor is invisible, or use indirect signs as proof of its boundary.

**direct** — [Imaging diagnosis and staging of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma: a comprehensive review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7183518/)

© The Author(s) 2020 · [License](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

> Early PDAC can be missed on CT due to similar attenuation relative to the normal pancreas, small size, or hidden location in the uncinate process.

**direct** — [Imaging diagnosis and staging of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma: a comprehensive review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7183518/)

© The Author(s) 2020 · [License](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

> Small isoattenuating PDAC, which can only manifest as a main pancreatic duct (MPD) stricture, can be easily missed on CT [7].

**direct** — [Imaging diagnosis and staging of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma: a comprehensive review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7183518/)

© The Author(s) 2020 · [License](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

> However, small and isoattenuating PDACs are challenging and can be overlooked on CT with a reported lower sensitivity of 58–77% for the detection of small (≤ 2 cm) tumors [16–18].

**direct** — [Imaging diagnosis and staging of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma: a comprehensive review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7183518/)

© The Author(s) 2020 · [License](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

> This advantage is based on accentuating the attenuation difference between the hypovascular tumor and the surrounding parenchyma [21].

## Could the size of the pancreas head indicate any specific diseases?

Case: q:dev6-31

**Expected meaning**

- Describe reported inflammatory enlargement as a general association.
- Distinguish focal inflammatory head masses from a numerical head-size threshold.
- Preserve overlap between inflammatory mass appearances and cancer.
- State the source limitation on an individual imaging feature.

**Limit:** General source-qualified disease context only. No current-scan diagnosis, numeric size-to-disease threshold, patient probability, treatment recommendation or imaging findings inferred from an outline. Preserve inflammation subtype, modality and phase; combinations and overlap matter.

**direct** — [The role of imaging in acute pancreatitis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8292294/)

© The Author(s) 2021 · [License](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

> IEP is more common and represents non necrotizing inflammation of the pancreas. Most patients, above 69%, have diffuse enlargement of the pancreas, occasionally it is localized, due to inflammatory edema.

**direct** — [Mass-forming pancreatitis versus pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma: CT and MR imaging for differentiation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7376657/)

© The Author(s) 2020 · [License](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

> The imaging appearance of MFCP has been described in detail [6, 17–19]. The typical features of MFCP at MDCT are a hypoattenuating mass at unenhanced CT, which is hypovascular on contrast-enhanced scans (Fig. 1). Masses are most often located in the pancreatic head, but can also be found in the body or tail of the pancreas (Fig. 2).

**direct** — [Mass-forming pancreatitis versus pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma: CT and MR imaging for differentiation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7376657/)

© The Author(s) 2020 · [License](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

> Autoimmune pancreatitis (AIP) is a rare form of chronic pancreatitis, which is characterized by lymphoplasmacytic infiltration with interstitial fibrosis, elevated serum IgG4 levels and a marked clinical response to corticosteroid therapy [37, 38]. AIP may present as with focal, multifocal, or diffuse infiltration of the gland (Fig. 11). The focal mass-forming type of AIP is present in 31–50% [39, 40]. Focal AIP can mimic PDAC, resulting in unnecessary pancreatic surgery for suspected cancer [41].

**direct** — [Mass-forming pancreatitis versus pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma: CT and MR imaging for differentiation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7376657/)

© The Author(s) 2020 · [License](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

> There is no single imaging feature that allows reliable differentiation between MFCP and PDAC or even PDAC developing CP.

**direct** — [Imaging diagnosis of autoimmune pancreatitis: computed tomography and magnetic resonance imaging](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8578112/)

© The Author(s) 2021 · [License](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

> Autoimmune pancreatitis (AIP) was first reported by Yoshida et al. [1] in 1995 as a disease that presents diffuse pancreatic swelling and tapered pancreatic ductal stenosis, ameliorated by steroid administration.

**direct** — [Imaging diagnosis and staging of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma: a comprehensive review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7183518/)

© The Author(s) 2020 · [License](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

> In diffuse AIP, the pancreas is diffusely enlarged “sausage shape” with loss of pancreatic lobulation and a capsule-like rim “halo” of hypoattenuation on CT or hypointensity on MR imaging [78].

**direct** — [Imaging diagnosis and staging of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma: a comprehensive review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7183518/)

© The Author(s) 2020 · [License](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

> Focal “mass-forming” AIP is a less common type and appears, similar to PDAC, as an irregular ill-defined mass-like abnormality; therefore, it is quite challenging to differentiate both entities.

**direct** — [Mass-forming pancreatitis versus pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma: CT and MR imaging for differentiation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7376657/)

© The Author(s) 2020 · [License](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

> Likewise focal autoimmune pancreatitis (AIP) and paraduodenal pancreatitis may present with imaging appearances not easily distinguishable from PDAC.

**partial_context** — [Imaging diagnosis of autoimmune pancreatitis: computed tomography and magnetic resonance imaging](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8578112/)

## By volume, how large is the pancreas on average? Give a published example, with its limits.

Case: q:dev2-10

**Expected meaning**

- One MRI study reported 82.6 +/- 4.8 cm3 in its normal-glucose-tolerance comparison group; statistics are mean +/- SEM, not SD.
- Only 14 normal-glucose-tolerance controls matched for age, weight and sex; MRI study context and small selected group limit generalization to a universal average or CT patient comparison.

**Limit:** Small MRI control-group mean, not a universal population average, CT reference interval, or current-patient normality threshold. SEM is not SD.

**partial_context** — [Imaging diagnosis of autoimmune pancreatitis: computed tomography and magnetic resonance imaging](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8578112/)

© The Author(s) 2021 · [License](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

> The definition of pancreatic swelling has classically been dependent on the criteria proposed by Haaga et al. [5]. Still, in reality, it is better to consider the age and background of the patient comprehensively.

## What additional findings help distinguish inflammation from a pancreatic tumor?

Case: q:dev6-32

**Expected meaning**

- Preserve the limitation that a combination of findings matters.
- Describe the reported inflammatory feature combination without assigning it to a scan.
- Describe cited secondary signs in the PDAC source context.
- Keep contrast-phase and autoimmune-pancreatitis context attached to the CT claim.
- Keep MRCP evidence labeled as MRCP, not a finding measurable from any CT outline.

**Limit:** General source-qualified disease context only. No current-scan diagnosis, numeric size-to-disease threshold, patient probability, treatment recommendation or imaging findings inferred from an outline. Preserve inflammation subtype, modality and phase; combinations and overlap matter.

**direct** — [Mass-forming pancreatitis versus pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma: CT and MR imaging for differentiation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7376657/)

© The Author(s) 2020 · [License](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

> There is no single imaging feature that allows reliable differentiation between MFCP and PDAC or even PDAC developing CP. It is a combination of features, which are either present or absent, which may drive you towards one or the other diagnosis.

**direct** — [Mass-forming pancreatitis versus pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma: CT and MR imaging for differentiation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7376657/)

© The Author(s) 2020 · [License](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

> MFCP patients likely show diffuse spotted or coarse parenchymal calcifications and/or ductal calcifications with duct-penetrating sign.

**direct** — [Imaging diagnosis and staging of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma: a comprehensive review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7183518/)

© The Author(s) 2020 · [License](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

> Early detection of subtle PDAC can be improved by identifying secondary signs that are seen in the majority (88%) of small isoattenuating tumors. The secondary signs include the following: (a) abrupt cut-off of MPD with or without upstream ductal dilatation; (b) distal pancreatic atrophy; (c) irregular pancreatic contour at the site of the tumor; (d) dilated MPD and CBD “double duct” sign; and (e) vascular encasement or narrowing [7, 22].

**direct** — [Mass-forming pancreatitis versus pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma: CT and MR imaging for differentiation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7376657/)

© The Author(s) 2020 · [License](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

> Focal AIP usually shows homogenous enhancement on portal-venous phase CT, a smooth narrowing of the pancreatic duct at the site of the mass (no abrupt cut-off) with only mild main pancreatic duct dilatation (≤ 5 mm) upstream, and the absence of proximal pancreatic atrophy [41].

**direct** — [Imaging diagnosis of autoimmune pancreatitis: computed tomography and magnetic resonance imaging](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8578112/)

© The Author(s) 2021 · [License](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

> In AIP, the main pancreatic duct (MPD) may penetrate the lesion without complete occlusion. In addition, the MPD stenosis in the lesion may taper, which is called the icicle sign [18]. Multiple skip narrowings of the MPD may also be seen (Fig. 6b) [17]. Magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP), its depiction not depending on luminal contrast delivery upstream to the stenosis, is suitable for depicting the entire MPD pathologies affected by AIP.

**direct** — [Improvement of the Diagnosis of Isoattenuating Pancreatic Carcinomas by Defining Their Characteristics on Contrast Enhanced Computed Tomography and Endosonography with Fine-Needle Aspiration (EUS-FNA)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8145900/)

© 2021 by the authors. · [License](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

> Secondary signs of isoattenuating pancreatic carcinomas on CT were present in 95.2% cases and included dilatation of the pancreatic duct and/or the common bile duct (85.7%), interruption of the pancreatic duct (76.2%), abnormal pancreatic contour (33.3%), and atrophy of the distal parenchyma (9.5%)

**direct** — [Improvement of the Diagnosis of Isoattenuating Pancreatic Carcinomas by Defining Their Characteristics on Contrast Enhanced Computed Tomography and Endosonography with Fine-Needle Aspiration (EUS-FNA)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8145900/)

© 2021 by the authors. · [License](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

> The most common signs of isoattenuating pancreatic carcinomas on CT were dilatation of the pancreatic duct or CBD (85.7%) and interruption of the pancreatic duct (76.2%).

**contradicting_or_limiting** — [Improvement of the Diagnosis of Isoattenuating Pancreatic Carcinomas by Defining Their Characteristics on Contrast Enhanced Computed Tomography and Endosonography with Fine-Needle Aspiration (EUS-FNA)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8145900/)

© 2021 by the authors. · [License](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

> The aim of our study was to focus only on patients with a definitive diagnosis of pancreatic cancer, so we could not evaluate the differences in isoattenuating pancreatic carcinomas compared to other diagnoses.

**contradicting_or_limiting** — [Imaging diagnosis and staging of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma: a comprehensive review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7183518/)
